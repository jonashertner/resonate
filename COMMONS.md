# The newsstand, and what belongs on it

The newsstand is a shelf, not a feed. It holds folios that people have offered
and a person has read. It is small on purpose, and it is meant to stay worth
reading rather than to grow.

This page is what a folio is held to, and what a reader can expect of one.

## What the reader is owed

Every folio on the stand says four things about itself, and the app shows them
before the folio is opened:

- **who** wrote it, under a byline. A byline is a typed name, not a proof.
- **where they stand.** One line, in the author's own words, so a reader knows
  whose eyes these are: a decade of sunday mornings in one city is a different
  thing from a week's holiday, and both are worth reading if they say so.
- **whether they have been to every place in it.** Answered plainly, yes or no.
  A folio of places someone wants to go is a fine thing, and it is not the same
  thing.
- **when it was last reviewed**, so a reader can tell a living folio from one
  its author has not thought about in three years.

The order the shelves appear in, and the order within them, is computed on the
reader's own device from their own atlas. Nothing about that ranking leaves.
The method is at [/METHOD.md](https://resonate.select/METHOD.md).

## What belongs

Places the author would actually send a friend to, in a set that has a reason
to be one set. A folio is a slice, not an inventory: a hundred places with
nothing in common is a directory, and directories already exist.

## What does not

- **Anything the author has not stood in, presented as though they had.** Say
  so instead; it is allowed and it is honest.
- **A business listing itself,** or anyone paid to include a place. There is no
  advertising here and there will not be. A folio that turns out to be one comes
  down.
- **Affiliate links,** tracking parameters, or any address that follows a reader.
- **Someone's home,** or anyone else's, whether or not they are famous. The same
  goes for a shelter, a refuge, a clinic, or anywhere a person's presence is
  their own business.
- **Places that cannot survive being found.** A fragile site, a wild place with
  no path, a ruin that is standing because nobody goes there, a sacred place
  where visitors are not welcome. If publishing it would damage it, the folio is
  the wrong place for it.
- **Private land presented as open,** and anything requiring trespass to reach.

## Precision

A folio carries coordinates. Where a place is sensitive but publishable, the
author should say so in the note rather than sharpen the point. Any place or
way marked as never leaving in an author's own atlas is already excluded from
everything they offer, in every format. A way may also be asked to give up its
first and last quarter kilometre, which is where a person tends to live.

## How a folio arrives, and how it leaves

It arrives by being offered: the app puts the folio on the author's clipboard
and opens an issue on the commons repository, where a person reads it before
anything goes up. That reading is the whole quality control, and it is meant to
be.

It leaves the same way. The author asks, from the folio itself, and a person
removes it. There is no automatic retraction, because there is no automatic
publication. A copy someone has already taken into their own atlas stays
theirs, as it would with any link.

## Reporting something

If a folio breaks these standards, open an issue on the commons repository, or
write to the address in
[/.well-known/security.txt](https://resonate.select/.well-known/security.txt).
Anything naming a private individual or endangering a place is acted on before
it is argued about.

## Copyright

A folio is the author's own words about places that exist. Notes copied from
guidebooks, reviews, or other people's writing do not belong here. Places are
facts and cannot be owned; the sentences about them can.
